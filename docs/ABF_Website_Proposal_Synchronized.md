# **1. Why ABF Needs a Website**

Right now, most of what people know about ABF today lives in scattered
WhatsApp messages, word of mouth, and the memory of whoever happens to
be an executive or beneficiary that year.

A website fixes this. It gives ABF one permanent, official place online
where any student, staff member, or outside partner can go to see what
ABF is, what it has done, and how to get involved --- regardless of who
is currently running it.

*In short: the website is ABF\'s public face. It should answer "what is
this and how do I engage with them" in under a minute, for someone who
has never heard of ABF before.*

# **2. What the Website Will Do**

Think of the website as a digital front door with two jobs:

- Inform--- tell visitors what ABF is, what it has achieved, and why
  it\'s real and trustworthy.

- Direct --- get visitors to take one clear next step: join the WhatsApp
  community, follow ABF on social media, or reach out about becoming a
  beneficiary or volunteer.

Every page and feature described below exists to support one of those
two jobs.

# **3. What Will Be on the Website**

This section covers every feature, explained in plain terms.

## **3.1 Home Page**

The first thing a visitor sees. A short, confident summary of what ABF
is, a few featured beneficiary stories, and clear buttons ("Join the
Community," "See Our Stories") so visitors immediately know what to do
next.

## **3.2 History and Background (About)**

A dedicated page telling ABF\'s story --- how it started, its mission,
and what it stands for. This builds long-term trust, and helps ABF stand
apart from others, similarly named organizations.

## **3.3 What ABF Offers / Programmes**

A clear breakdown of what ABF provides: scholarships, skills training,
mentorship, and any other support --- written so a first-year student
immediately understands "what\'s in it for me."

## **3.4 Beneficiary Testimonials**

Real beneficiaries share their experience in whichever format they\'re
comfortable with: written, audio, or video. Audio/video files are stored
separately from the site\'s page code, so pages stay fast to load. This
is one of the most persuasive parts of the site, since its students hear
directly from other students rather than from ABF itself.

## **3.5 Beneficiaries Directory**

A simple, browsable directory of past and current beneficiaries (names,
and level/department where appropriate), pulled from the same database
as the rest of the site\'s content, so an executive can update it
without a developer. This adds transparency and credibility --- anyone
can verify that ABF is real and active, not just a claim on a page.

## **3.6 Activities / Events**

A section covering ABF\'s past and upcoming activities.

## **3.7 News / Updates**

A lightweight space for announcements, added where required rather than
as a heavy standalone section.

## **3.8 How to Join / Get Involved**

A short, no-confusion page or section explaining exactly what "joining
ABF" means and what the steps are, ending in a direct link to act.

## **3.9 Contact**

Official contact information plus a direct link into ABF\'s existing
volunteer/community Google Form --- the same one already being built for
the team, so there\'s no separate custom form and no duplicate data
entry.

## **3.10 Social Media and Community Links**

Visible, always accessible links to ABF\'s social media pages (TikTok,
Instagram, X) and --- most importantly --- direct links into the ABF
WhatsApp community. These appear in multiple places on the site, not
buried in one corner.

## **3.11 Call-to-Action (CTA) Buttons**

Buttons like "Join the Community" or "Share Your Story" placed
prominently across the site. These convert a visitor into a participant,
so they\'re treated as a core feature, not decoration.

## **3.12 Professional Branding**

The whole site uses ABF\'s official logo, colors, and fonts once
provided, so it looks and feels unmistakably like ABF --- consistent
with flyers, social posts, and other materials.

# **4. Design Priorities**

- Simple and easy to navigate --- no clutter, no confusing menus.

- Works well on phones --- most students will visit from a mobile
  device, not a laptop.

- Findable on Google --- so someone searching "Adeyinka Bakare
  Fellowship" finds the real, official site first, not an impersonator
  or nothing at all.

- Accessible and credible --- convinces a skeptical student or outside
  partner that ABF is legitimate.

# **6. Domain, Email & Infrastructure Setup**

As part of the project, the development team will handle the initial
technical setup, including:

- Domain availability check and purchase of an approved ABF domain.

- DNS configuration.

- Website hosting and deployment.

- Supabase database setup.

- Supabase storage configuration for website media.

- Administrative authentication setup.

- Transactional email configuration for admin/account notifications (not
  needed for the volunteer/contact form itself, which uses the existing
  Google Form).

- Linking ABF\'s existing Google Form and WhatsApp/social links into the
  site.

- Search engine and basic SEO configuration.

- Website analytics setup where required.

- Production testing and final deployment.

All purchased services and accounts will be registered using information
controlled by ABF where possible.

# **7. Secure Administration & Handover**

The website will include a secure administrative dashboard for
authorized ABF personnel. Depending on final scope, administrators will
be able to manage:

- Beneficiaries

- Testimonials

- Events

- Programmes

- News/updates

- Website statistics

- Uploaded media

The development team will provide ABF with the necessary account
details, administrative access, configuration information, and basic
instructions required to manage the platform securely. The goal is to
ensure the website remains under ABF\'s ownership and control, and does
not become dependent on the current developers.

# **9. Keeping the Website Updated (Maintenance)**

A website that goes stale after launch does more harm than good, so this
is treated as part of the proposal, not an afterthought.

- Content updates: New testimonials, beneficiary names, and impact
  numbers should be added at least once a month --- this takes minutes,
  not hours, using the admin dashboard described above.

- Ownership: We recommend formally assigning one executive or volunteer
  as the site maintainer, trained during handover, so the site doesn\'t
  depend on the original developers once their time on the team ends.

- Technical support: Prince and Damilare remain the technical point of
  contact for anything beyond routine content updates, such as design
  changes or new features.

# **14. Additional Suggestions to Maximize Impact**

- Add the website link to every flyer, social media bio, and email
  signature at the moment it launches, so it becomes the default place
  people are pointed to.

- Track simple visitor numbers (free tools exist for this) so ABF can
  see whether the website is driving people to join the community ---
  not just guess.

- Launch with at least 4--5 strong testimonials already in place; an
  empty or thin testimonials page undercuts the credibility the site is
  meant to build.

- Consider a short "official ABF" notice on the home page addressing
  similarly named organizations directly, to protect ABF\'s reputation.
